# Golden Crest Registration and Certification Roadmap

## Plain-language answer

Golden Crest does not need every certification at once. It needs the legal and operating foundation first, then the registrations connected to the actual buyers it plans to pursue.

## 1. Foundation required before certifications

1. Final legal business name.
2. North Carolina LLC filing.
3. Operating agreement signed by Miesha and Kimberly.
4. Free EIN from the IRS.
5. Dedicated business bank account.
6. Bookkeeping, W-9, and tax setup.
7. Business email, phone, and physical address records that match across systems.
8. Insurance matched to the first service scope.
9. Written service offer, pricing method, safety procedures, and staffing plan.

## 2. What an operating agreement is

An operating agreement is the LLC's internal rulebook. It is not a government certification.

For a 50/50 company, it should answer:

- Who owns what percentage.
- Who manages daily operations.
- Who can sign bids, contracts, checks, loans, leases, and insurance applications.
- How profits, losses, wages, and owner draws are handled.
- What each founder contributes in money, equipment, labor, or expertise.
- How decisions are approved.
- How a 50/50 deadlock is resolved.
- What happens if a founder leaves, becomes sick, stops working, dies, divorces, or wants to sell.
- How a buyout price is calculated.
- Who owns the brand, website, SOPs, customer list, and equipment.
- Whether Derrick is an employee, contractor, officer, or non-owner business-development adviser.

For WOSB or EDWOSB purposes, the documents and real operation must show that qualifying women own at least 51% and control daily and long-term decisions. Miesha and Kimberly can collectively satisfy the ownership requirement if both are qualifying women, but the agreement should not quietly give Derrick or another non-qualifying person veto or control rights.

A North Carolina business attorney should review the final 50/50 agreement because deadlock and exit language can determine whether the company survives a disagreement.

## 3. Required vendor registrations by market

### North Carolina state opportunities

- Register as a vendor in NC eVP.
- Request NCSBE certification if the company meets the program rules.
- The old state HUB program ended in July 2026. State contracts no longer recognize HUB certification. NCSBE is the current race- and gender-neutral state small-business path.[11]

### City of Charlotte opportunities

- Register as a City of Charlotte supplier.
- Apply for Charlotte SBE certification if the business meets the significant-presence, ownership, size, and personal-net-worth rules.[7][8]
- Gaston County is within the Charlotte program's listed regional footprint, but the company must still satisfy every application requirement.

### Gaston County and other local buyers

- Register for bid notifications and vendor onboarding required by each county, town, school, college, housing authority, or public entity.
- Local registration is separate from SAM.

### Federal opportunities

- Register free at SAM.gov for All Awards.
- SAM issues the UEI and routes a U.S. entity through CAGE assignment.
- Keep the registration active and renew it every 365 days.[15]
- Build the Small Business Search profile with accurate NAICS codes and capabilities.

## 4. Women-owned and minority certifications

### WOSB: legitimate and free through SBA

**WOSB means Women-Owned Small Business.** It is a real SBA federal contracting certification.

The free application is at MySBA Certifications. SBA states that it does not charge to apply to its programs.[16]

Basic requirements include:

- Small under the relevant SBA size standard.
- At least 51% directly and unconditionally owned and controlled by one or more women who are U.S. citizens.
- Women manage daily operations and long-term decisions.
- Active SAM registration and matching business records.

WOSB certification makes the business eligible to compete for WOSB program set-asides in eligible industries. It does not guarantee first choice, an award, or payment.

### EDWOSB: legitimate and free through SBA

**EDWOSB means Economically Disadvantaged Women-Owned Small Business.** It includes all WOSB requirements plus personal financial limits for the qualifying women.

Current SBA thresholds include:

- Personal net worth of $850,000 or less.
- Adjusted gross income averaging $400,000 or less.
- Personal assets totaling $6.5 million or less.[4]

Janitorial Services, NAICS 561720, is currently designated on SBA's eligible-industry list under EDWOSB rather than ordinary WOSB.[10] This is why Golden Crest should evaluate EDWOSB honestly. If Miesha and Kimberly do not meet the economic tests, Golden Crest can still pursue ordinary small-business opportunities and other work even if janitorial set-asides are not available under the WOSB program.

### Paid third-party WOSB certification is optional

SBA allows approved third-party certifiers, but the company can use SBA's free process instead. A company that pays an approved third-party certifier still has to submit the required documentation through SBA's system for federal WOSB program participation.

Do not pay an unknown consultant to "get" a federal certification. Use `certifications.sba.gov`, SAM.gov, SBA, SBTDC, or APEX. None can guarantee approval or contracts.

### WBENC WBE: legitimate, paid, and mainly corporate

WBENC's WBE certification is a legitimate private-sector supplier-diversity credential. It is optional, not required for SAM or the free SBA application.

WBENC currently lists a $350 application fee for businesses with annual revenue under $1 million, with higher tiers as revenue increases.[17] It may be valuable only when named corporate buyers or prime contractors actively use WBENC sourcing. Do not buy it just to decorate the website.

### NMSDC MBE: legitimate, paid, and mainly corporate

NMSDC's MBE certification is a legitimate private-sector minority-business credential. It may be relevant if the company is at least 51% minority-owned, controlled, and operated under NMSDC rules.

NMSDC says initial fees vary by business size and can be as low as $270 for businesses under $1 million in revenue.[18] Like WBENC, it is useful when target corporations or prime contractors actually source through that network. It does not guarantee work.

## 5. Recommended order for Golden Crest

1. Finalize the founders' ownership and operating agreement.
2. Form the LLC, obtain EIN, bank account, insurance, and monitored contact channels.
3. Establish the initial commercial offer and cost model.
4. Request no-cost APEX and NC SBTDC Government Contracting Assistance Program guidance for registration, capture planning, procurement compliance, and account targeting.[19][20]
5. Build a named-account list of agencies, institutions, prime contractors, and facility buyers.
6. Register for Gaston and nearby local vendor opportunities.
7. Register in NC eVP and apply for NCSBE if eligible.[11]
8. Register as a City of Charlotte supplier and evaluate Charlotte SBE.[7][8]
9. Register free in SAM.gov and complete the Small Business Search profile.[5][15]
10. Apply free through MySBA Certifications for WOSB and EDWOSB if the founders meet the requirements.[4][16]
11. Buy WBENC or NMSDC only after a real target buyer says the credential affects sourcing.

## 6. What belongs on the website

### Hero

The hero should sell the service, not the certification stack:

- What Golden Crest does.
- What types of facilities it serves.
- Why the founders' hospitality operations background matters.
- One primary CTA: request a site walkthrough.

A simple factual phrase such as "women-led" can appear when accurate. Do not place WOSB, EDWOSB, WBE, MBE, SBE, SAM, UEI, or CAGE badges in the hero before approval.

### Government readiness section

Display:

- NAICS 561720.
- PSC S201.
- Legal business name.
- Service area.
- UEI and CAGE after issuance.
- Active SAM status and renewal date.
- Exact certification name, issuer, identifier, and expiration after approval.
- Insurance and bonding information only after bound.
- Downloadable capability statement.

The NAICS and PSC codes are classification and search data. They are not certifications.

### Capability statement

The capability statement is the detailed procurement-facing document. It should contain codes, identifiers, core capabilities, differentiators, key-person experience, contact information, and verified past performance. The website should link to it rather than forcing every code into the hero.

## 7. Photo-assisted walkthrough tool

A photo-assisted scope-review tool is a good idea. It should be built as a real intake workflow, not displayed as operational before it exists.

Safe first version:

1. Buyer chooses on-site walkthrough or photo-assisted review.
2. Buyer provides basic contact and facility information.
3. Buyer accepts privacy and document-handling terms.
4. Secure upload accepts ordinary room photos and non-sensitive scope documents.
5. Files go to private storage, not a public website directory.
6. Golden Crest receives a monitored alert and reviews the submission.
7. The system produces a draft scope checklist for human review, not an automatic binding quote.
8. Retention and deletion rules remove files after the review period.

Do not request alarm codes, door codes, access credentials, employee data, restricted floor plans, or sensitive government documents through the marketing site.

Derrick's VIN and car-lot workflow proves that a photo-driven intake can be built. Golden Crest still needs its own security model, data fields, storage boundary, review workflow, and acceptance testing.
