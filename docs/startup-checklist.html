            Everything that needs a phone call

            Grouped so they can all be done on one weekday. Most take ten minutes.

            Call-day list

            • APEX Accelerator — book the free procurement counselor. Do this one first; everything else gets easier after it.
            • NC SBTDC — the Charlotte office has a government contracting counselor. Same kind of free help.
            • Insurance agent — get two or three general liability quotes for commercial janitorial.
            • Bank — ask what they need to open a business account for an LLC, then book the appointment.
            • Town halls — Mount Holly, Belmont, Cramerton, Lowell, Stanley, Dallas, Bessemer City. Ask the clerk or finance office: "How do we get on your vendor list for cleaning?" Small towns handle this by phone, not by portal.
            • Housing authorities — ask the same question.
            • Gaston County Schools — confirm what their vendor packet needs beyond the online registration.
            Reference

            Things that catch new companies out

            Worth reading once. None of these are reasons not to do it — they are reasons to price and pace it properly.

            You get paid slowly

            Government and institutional buyers usually pay 30 to 60 days after you invoice. While it's just the two of you doing the cleaning, that is not a payroll crisis — but it does mean you could work a full month or two before the first check lands, while buying supplies up front.

            Start with a contract size you can afford to float, and know the payment terms before signing.

            Federal work sets your wage — the day you hire someone

            Most federal service contracts fall under the Service Contract Act, which means the Department of Labor sets the minimum wage for janitors in your county, plus a health & welfare benefit of $5.92 per hour as of August 2026. That is about $12,000 a year on top of wages for one full-time cleaner.

            While you are the only two people working, this matters much less. The moment you hire your first employee onto a federal or federally-funded job, it applies — and it also applies if a larger contractor hires you onto one of theirs. Never price that work at your normal commercial rate.

            Some federal cleaning cannot be bid by anyone

            A lot of federal building cleaning is reserved under the AbilityOne program for nonprofits employing people who are blind or severely disabled. When a building is on that list, the agency must buy from the assigned nonprofit — no competition, and no certification changes it.

            This is why federal work is the long game and not the first move. Ask the APEX counselor which facilities near us are not covered.

            Where being women-owned actually helps

            It helps a lot with federal contracts and with getting onto a large contractor's subcontractor list — those have real goals and set-asides attached.

            It does not help at Gaston County, the City of Gastonia, or Gaston County Schools. Local buying is mostly lowest responsible bidder, and North Carolina's state program is gender- and race-neutral now. Those you win by being registered, local, reliable and priced right.
