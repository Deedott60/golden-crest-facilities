## Executive decision

The business concept makes sense with one major correction: government contracting should be a parallel growth channel, not the only launch plan.

The strongest foundation is not a certification badge. It is the founders' experience managing sanitation, guest-facing presentation, teams, and compliance inside high-volume casino and resort operations. Golden Crest should first turn that experience into a narrow, repeatable commercial service. It can build government readiness at the same time.

## Project boundary

Golden Crest is a separate potential business for Miesha and Kimberly. It is not a LeadCurate product and it should have its own legal entity, bank account, insurance, contracts, records, website, and future operating system.

## Ownership and partnership warning

A 50/50 company can still be collectively 100% women-owned, but a 50/50 operating agreement can deadlock. Before filing, the founders should put these items in writing:

1. Who manages day-to-day operations.
2. Who can sign contracts and spend company funds.
3. Initial capital and equipment contributions.
4. Work expectations and compensation.
5. How disagreements are resolved.
6. What happens if one founder leaves, becomes unavailable, or wants to sell.
7. Who owns customer relationships, SOPs, brand assets, and equipment.
8. A buyout and dissolution method.

Both founders should also review their current employers' outside-business, conflict-of-interest, confidentiality, and non-solicitation policies before using employer experience in marketing.

## Corrected government-contracting facts

### Micro-purchases

The general federal micro-purchase threshold increased to $15,000 on October 1, 2025.[1] That does not mean every janitorial requirement under $15,000 can simply be placed on a card without other rules.

FAR 2.101 lists lower thresholds for some categories, including $2,500 for services covered by Service Contract Labor Standards and $2,000 for covered construction.[2] Recurring janitorial labor may be subject to the service-contract threshold and wage requirements depending on the acquisition.

Federal purchase cards are a preferred payment method for micro-purchases, and a qualified supplier may receive a micro-purchase without competitive quotations if the authorized buyer finds the price reasonable. Buyers should distribute micro-purchases among qualified suppliers when practicable.[3] A purchase card is therefore a useful payment capability, not a guaranteed sales channel.

### Simplified acquisitions

The general simplified acquisition threshold is now $350,000.[1] Acquisitions above the applicable micro-purchase threshold and at or below the simplified acquisition threshold often receive small-business consideration, but the plan should not claim that every requirement is automatically awarded to a small business or women-owned firm. The contracting officer still applies required-source rules, market research, responsibility, pricing, competition, and the solicitation's specific set-aside.

### SAM, UEI, and CAGE

SAM.gov registration and the Unique Entity ID are free.[5] Golden Crest should register only after its legal name, physical address, EIN, banking, and formation records match. A U.S. entity can receive a CAGE code through the SAM registration process. SAM says activation can take up to 10 business days and active registrations must be renewed every 365 days.[15]

### WOSB and EDWOSB

SBA certification requires a qualifying small business to be at least 51% owned and controlled by women who are U.S. citizens, with women managing day-to-day operations and long-term decisions.[4]

Janitorial Services, NAICS 561720, appears on SBA's eligible-industry list under the EDWOSB designation.[10] That means the founders should have an APEX or SBA counselor verify whether they meet the economic-disadvantage tests before building the federal strategy around women-owned janitorial set-asides. Certification does not create demand and does not replace past performance, pricing, staffing, insurance, or contract compliance.

### Local programs

North Carolina eliminated the state HUB program in July 2026. State contracts no longer recognize HUB certifications, pending HUB applications are not considered, and HUB certifications are not being renewed. The state directs new small businesses toward the race- and gender-neutral NCSBE program through eVP.[11]

The City of Charlotte requires supplier registration before a supplier can receive purchase orders or payment.[7] As of 2026, Charlotte describes its certification program as race- and gender-neutral SBE certification rather than local MBE or WBE certification.[8] A Mount Holly business may fall within the Charlotte Combined Statistical Area, but it must still satisfy the city's ownership, size, presence, and personal-net-worth requirements.

Gaston County has previously issued janitorial solicitations for individual facilities and countywide services.[9] Past postings prove the county buys the service. They do not prove a current open bid.

The NC SBTDC Charlotte office provides government-contracting assistance and lists a government contracting counselor.[6] This is the most direct local starting point for free registration, capability-statement, pricing, bid, and opportunity guidance.

## Corrected go-to-market strategy

### Lane 1: Commercial proof first

Start with one narrow offer:

> Founder-led recurring janitorial care for small professional, administrative, and public-facing facilities in Gaston and western Mecklenburg counties.

Best first targets:

- Professional offices.
- Administrative and nonprofit offices.
- Small municipal or community facilities when a vendor path is available.
- Hospitality-adjacent offices and public areas.
- One-time turnover and event reset work with a defined scope.

Do not lead with medical or clinical sanitation until the company has the required training, exposure-control plan, chemical procedures, insurance, and contract-specific capability.

### Lane 2: Local and state procurement

After formation and insurance:

1. Register with Gaston County bid notifications.
2. Register in North Carolina eVP and evaluate NCSBE eligibility.[11]
3. Register as a City of Charlotte supplier.[7]
4. Evaluate Charlotte SBE eligibility.[8]
5. Monitor school, college, housing, municipal, county, and nonprofit facility opportunities.
6. Pursue subcontracting under established janitorial primes where past performance is required.

### Lane 3: Federal readiness

1. Form the company and obtain EIN, bank account, W-9, insurance, and documented ownership.
2. Register free in SAM.gov and obtain UEI and CAGE.[5]
3. Build the SBA small-business profile and accurate NAICS list.
4. Ask SBTDC or APEX to verify WOSB or EDWOSB eligibility and the 561720 strategy.
5. Respond to sources-sought notices that fit real capacity.
6. Target small, local, clearly scoped RFQs and subcontracting before large multi-site requirements.
7. Use purchase-card acceptance as a convenience after a buyer identifies a need. Do not market it as automatic access to cardholders.

## Initial operating offer

### Included at launch

- Recurring office and administrative janitorial service.
- Restroom, breakroom, common-area, floor, glass, waste, and high-touch cleaning within a written scope.
- Public-area opening or closing readiness.
- One-time turnover or event reset cleaning.
- Founder-led startup checklist and quality review.
- Documented issue escalation and consumable observations.

### Not claimed until built and verified

- Medical-grade or infection-control services.
- Bonding.
- Background-cleared staff.
- A dedicated crew.
- Two-hour response guarantees.
- QR room systems or tamper-proof audit logs.
- A live customer portal.
- Automated photo bidding.
- Green Seal products.
- EPA, SAM, UEI, CAGE, WOSB, EDWOSB, WBE, MBE, or SBE status.
- Any specific insurance limit.

## Pricing method

Do not publish generic square-foot pricing yet. Build pricing from:

- Labor hours by task and frequency.
- Founders' or employees' loaded labor cost.
- Payroll taxes and workers' compensation.
- Supplies, equipment, laundry, and consumables.
- Travel and access time.
- Supervision and quality-control time.
- Insurance and administrative overhead.
- Contract-specific wage determinations.
- A profit and contingency allowance.

Every proposal should separate recurring base scope, periodic work, supplies, floor care, windows, emergency calls, and one-time add-ons.

North Carolina generally requires workers' compensation coverage when an employer regularly has three or more employees, although customers or prime contractors may require it sooner.[12] Commercial general liability, vehicle-related coverage, equipment/property coverage, workers' compensation, and bonding should be quoted against the actual service model. A janitorial fidelity bond and a contract bid, performance, or payment bond solve different risks and should not be advertised interchangeably.

## 90-day launch sequence

### Days 1 to 15: formation decisions

- Confirm legal name and service area.
- Confirm the founders' ownership, duties, availability, capital, and deadlock plan.
- Review employer policies.
- Obtain insurance quotes before making public claims.
- Price one real sample facility from a site walkthrough.

### Days 16 to 30: legal and operational foundation

- File the entity after attorney or qualified adviser review. North Carolina currently lists a $125 Articles of Organization filing fee, and LLC annual reports are currently $200 plus any applicable online transaction fee.[13][14]
- Obtain EIN, bank account, bookkeeping, W-9, and insurance.
- Purchase only the equipment needed for the first service lane.
- Create chemical, SDS, PPE, key-control, incident, and quality-check procedures.

### Days 31 to 60: commercial proof

- Pursue three to five carefully selected local facilities.
- Win one small recurring account or clearly scoped one-time project.
- Document the approved scope, checklist, issue process, and customer reference.
- Measure actual labor time and margin.

### Days 61 to 90: procurement readiness

- Meet the Charlotte NC SBTDC government-contracting counselor.[6]
- Register in the appropriate local and state vendor systems.
- Complete SAM only when records match.[5]
- Validate WOSB or EDWOSB eligibility before applying.[4][10]
- Finalize a one-page capability statement with only active credentials and proven past performance.
- Begin sources-sought, subcontractor, and small RFQ outreach.

## Website decision

The website should lead with service quality and founder experience. Government identifiers belong in a procurement-readiness section and printable capability profile, not as decorative hero badges. Pending registrations should be labeled pending. They should never be displayed as active credentials.

The rebuilt `index.html` follows this rule. The printable `capability-statement.html` is a pre-registration draft and can be finalized when the legal business name, contact details, insurance, UEI, CAGE, certification status, service radius, and references are real.
